## Change

`crates/engine/src/net/liveness.rs::keyless_re_put` now resolves each held name through `fanout_get_classified` before it re-PUTs, and re-PUTs the freshest record: the network copy when its sequence is strictly higher, else the held copy. `Absent` and `Unavailable` both keep the held record. A held record whose signature does not chain to its own routing key is skipped rather than re-PUT, which is the produce-side mirror of what the resolve side rejects. Signature unchanged; the facade needed no wiring change.

On the adoption gate: the pass adopts nothing, touches no snapshot, and raises no floor, so no adopter is reachable or needed at that layer. The record-plane verify (core's Ed25519 chain from the name) is enforced on every candidate, and every record that reaches the snapshot still passes the full gate on the resolve tick. A party who can sign a higher-sequence record under a name can PUT it to every endpoint directly, so the pass amplifies nothing.

## The held-set rule, as stated at `HeldRecords`

A record enters the set only where the session proved it current: the vault root from the resolve tick's gate-passing resolve (the only record a resolve holds, so a child the session did not publish itself never enters); any node the session published, from the drain's confirmed publish; an owned scope pointer from a confirmed mint or re-point flip and from the hourly re-enrolment (a grantee derives neither that name nor its signer). The set is session memory: it starts empty, is never persisted, and is cleared at teardown. The duplicate restatement on the `Engine::held_records` field was reduced to a cross-reference.

This also answers the issue's question 1: the web's publishes did enter the set, but the set lives in the leader tab's worker heap and dies with it, and a resolve only ever re-holds the vault root, so a published child is never re-held after that worker goes away.
