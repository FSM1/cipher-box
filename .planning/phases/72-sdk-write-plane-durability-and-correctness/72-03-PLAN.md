<objective>
Deliver SC#1: `deleteItem` drops the removed child's `WriteChildRef` in the same CAS publish that removes its read-plane `SealedChildRef`, keyed by node UUID — with no unbounded write-chain growth AND no resurrection under a concurrent-write race.

This plan pairs two coupled pieces because SC#1 is not correct without both:
1. The write-body CAS-merge in `updateFolderMetadataAndPublish` is today a naive `byChildId` union with NO base-diff and NO deletion-pruning (RESEARCH Critical Finding 2). Its own comment admits it only works because "deletes are preserved verbatim" — the exact premise SC#1 falsifies. Once `deleteItem` drops a WriteChildRef, a concurrent writer hitting the 409 retry path re-unions the stale entry back in and RESURRECTS it. This must be made base-aware first.
2. `deleteItem` must resolve the removed item's UUID and filter it out of `writeChildren` — its `childId` param is an ipnsName, NOT the UUID `WriteChildRef.childId` needs (RESEARCH Pitfall 1), so a naive filter silently no-ops.

Scope decision (Open Question 1, resolved): `deleteItem` (hard delete, no bin) DROPS. `addToBin` (soft delete) RETAINS the WriteChildRef unchanged (so Plan 05's restoreFromBin can re-home it) — documented, not changed here. The permanent-delete-drop-from-original-parent path is handled in Plan 05 where the original-parent load plumbing lives.

Purpose: Stop write-body growth on delete without introducing a concurrency regression.
Output: base-aware write-body merge, UUID-keyed delete drop, and two regression tests.
</objective>
