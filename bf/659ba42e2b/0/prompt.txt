## Key finding — the review gates caught three real bugs, all latent behind the fakes

1. **HIGH, pre-existing in `reseal.rs`:** the write body's structure signature was signed at the **write** epoch while the gate recomputes every structure preimage at the **read** epoch. ## The (a)-vs-(b) decision: **(b)**, and one of the issue's premises is wrong

The issue's option (a) — "the gate authenticates the write-body at the record's authenticated write epoch (carried in the section/envelope)" — **is not implementable as described. **The part worth your attention:** my first cut did exactly what CodeRabbit asked — `Err` on a chain that won't walk — and **that was a denial-of-revocation vector.** Both review gates caught it independently. The gate authenticates each link's signature and nothing about their order, so a committed write-grantee can publish an adopted root with reordered links; the owner's revocation rotation then fails closed **permanently**, and on the cascade path it's non-retryable and aborts mid-walk, leaving a revocation partially applied. Before this branch that input rotated fine.

So it now **truncates**: keep the walkable suffix, drop everything older. Nothing unwalkable is published — no reader could walk past that break either — so the fail-closed property is preserved while the attacker loses the ability to block their own removal. Walk is bounded to the retained window; epochs must strictly decrease.

Also folded in: a release-active entry bound mirroring the codec's (so a non-pruning sweep can't spend a section of signatures on a set the encoder then refuses), and two corrections to my own earlier prose — a sweep *does* hold the current seed (what blocks it is the missing minting epoch), and a link is sealed under **its own** epoch's key while *carrying* the preceding epoch's seed.

**Residual, recorded in the blueprint:** a sweep no longer trims an oversized-but-gate-valid set, so only a rotation heals it. Bounded and stated rather than papered over. No new issue filed — this is a documented property of the design, not deferred work.
